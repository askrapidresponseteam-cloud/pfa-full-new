Laws

Animal laws.

Before you cite any of this. Every answer names the provision it rests on, so you can check it rather than take it on trust. Read the section on indiacode.nic.in before you quote it, and check your State: prevention of cruelty is a concurrent subject, so State rules and municipal by-laws sit on top of everything here. A section cited wrongly in an FIR is harder to correct than one left out. Last read against the statutes in August 2026.

Showing every question that rests on

Dogs

A1Which law protects dogs from cruelty?

Two provisions, always read together.

Section 3 of the PCA Act 1960 puts a positive duty on anyone in charge of an animal to prevent unnecessary pain, so inaction is itself actionable. Section 11(1) then lists the offences: beating, kicking, overloading, starving, confining, abandoning and more.

Basis
A2Which section carries a real sentence for cruelty?

Where the animal is killed, poisoned, maimed or rendered useless, BNS Section 325 carries up to five years and is a cognizable offence, so the police are obliged to register an FIR rather than take a complaint and sit on it. It applies to any animal, owned or not, and the old value thresholds in IPC 428 and 429 are gone. Charge it there. The PCA Act alone is not worth filing under: its penalty has never been raised from the 1960 figures, and the 2022 amendment that would raise it is still not enacted.

CarefulSection 325 is mischief, so it needs an intentional act: starving, neglect and failure to treat are not mischief, and belong under PCA Section 11 read with Section 3. And it is bailable, so expect the accused to be released; the value of the section is that it forces registration and carries a real sentence at trial, not that it holds anyone.

Basis
A3Is killing or maiming a dog a crime?

Yes, and it is the serious charge.

BNS Section 325, which replaced IPC 428 and 429, punishes killing, poisoning, maiming or rendering useless any animal with up to five years, or fine, or both.

Do thisInsist the FIR names Section 325 and not merely the PCA Act.

Basis
A4Can one be jailed for poisoning community dogs?

Yes.

Section 11(1)(l) of the PCA Act protects street dogs by name from being poisoned with strychnine or harmed by any other unnecessarily cruel method. Add BNS Section 325 for the custodial sentence. Ask for a post mortem, because the toxicology report is the evidence the case rests on.

Basis
A5What are the ABC Rules 2023?

Rules framed under Section 38 of the PCA Act for managing community dogs: humane capture, sterilisation and vaccination, then return of the dog to the exact place it came from. Return to its own locality is the rule, not a concession. The local body must run the programme through an AWBI recognised organisation.

Basis
A6Who is responsible for community dogs in my area?

The local body, meaning your municipal corporation, city municipal council or gram panchayat.

The ABC Rules 2023 place the duty to run sterilisation and anti rabies vaccination on it, not on residents or feeders. If it has no ABC programme, that is a default you can act on in writing.

Basis
A7Can community dogs be relocated?

As a rule, no.

The ABC Rules require a sterilised and vaccinated dog to go back to its own locality, and relocation is not a lawful method of managing community dogs. The Supreme Court order of 19 May 2026 made a narrow exception for premises such as schools, hospitals, sports complexes, bus stands and railway stations: dogs from there are sterilised, vaccinated and given safe placement in a designated shelter, where they are to be cared for.

Do thisRead that as a narrow exception, not a licence, and ask that any dog placed in a shelter receives proper care there.

CarefulThis is a continuing suo motu matter now monitored by every High Court, so check the current position before relying on it.

Basis
A8What did the Supreme Court's May 2026 judgment say?

That the ABC Rules remain the framework for humane management of community dogs, through sterilisation and vaccination; that dogs from schools, hospitals and similar premises are to be sterilised, vaccinated and given safe placement in a designated shelter; that every district must have at least one working ABC centre; and that officials who do not follow the directions are answerable. It also upheld the AWBI’s standard operating procedure, which is the document a municipality works from. The mandatory district centre is the part most useful to citizens and to the animals.

CarefulThe proceedings continue and High Courts are monitoring compliance, so directions can move.

Basis
A9When may a community dog be humanely euthanised?

Only on three grounds under the 2026 directions, each to be assessed by qualified veterinary experts: rabies, an incurable illness causing suffering, or severe aggression established on evidence. A resident complaint is not a ground. It must be done humanely by a registered veterinarian. Anything else is an offence under BNS Section 325.

CarefulThe judgment says “rabid”, not “laboratory confirmed”, and the safeguard it names is assessment by qualified veterinary experts. Ask for that assessment on record rather than for a lab report the order does not require.

Basis
A10Is feeding community dogs legal?

Yes, and obstructing a feeder is the actionable conduct, not the feeding.

The ABC Rules 2023 require resident associations and local bodies to designate feeding spots. The Delhi High Court in Dr Maya D. Chablani v. Radha Mittal (2021) held that community dogs have a right to food and that citizens have a right to feed them, subject to using designated points and keeping them clean.

Basis
A11Can my apartment RWA ban pets or the feeding of community animals?

No.

A resident association is a private body and cannot override rules made under a central Act. AWBI guidelines and repeated High Court rulings hold that an RWA cannot ban pets, bar them from lifts, or prohibit feeding. It may regulate reasonably, meaning time and place, and nothing more.

CarefulThe May 2026 judgment read “gated campus” in the ABC Rules narrowly, so a society enclosing a school, hospital or similar premises may stand differently from an ordinary residential block.

Basis
A12Can a housing society fine me for my dog barking?

It can ask for reasonable steps.

Barking is not a ground for sending the animal away, and AWBI guidance says so directly. A resolution passed by a majority does not become lawful merely because it was passed.

Basis
A13Do I need a dog licence?

It depends on your local body, since this is a municipal by-law subject and not a central one.

Many corporations require annual registration of pet dogs. Check Udupi City Municipal Council for the local position. Registration is also the quickest way to bring your dog home if it is ever lost or taken in by the municipality.

Basis
A14Is rabies vaccination compulsory?

In practice, yes.

Rabies is notifiable under the PCICDA 2009, and annual anti rabies vaccination is required for municipal registration, for travel, and for the National Action Plan for Dog Mediated Rabies Elimination by 2030.

Do thisKeep the certificate, since it is what protects you if a bite is alleged.

Basis
A15Are Pitbulls and Rottweilers banned in India?

No valid nationwide ban exists.

A central letter of March 2024 asked States to prohibit 23 breeds. Within weeks six High Courts had set it aside or stayed it, two quashing it outright, for want of consultation with the people it affected and for treating breed as a proxy for danger.

Do thisCheck your own municipality, but a mere advisory is not law.

Basis
A16Do dog breeders need a licence?

Yes.

The Dog Breeding and Marketing Rules 2017 require registration with the State Animal Welfare Board, minimum housing and veterinary standards, and record keeping. A female may not be bred below 18 months or more than once a year. An unregistered breeder is operating illegally, and photographs of the premises are worth more than a complaint.

Basis
A17Do pet shops need a licence?

Yes.

The Pet Shop Rules 2018 require registration with the State Animal Welfare Board, space and veterinary norms, and prohibit the sale of animals under eight weeks old. Selling from an unregistered shop is an offence under the parent PCA Act.

Basis
A18Can one buy a puppy online or on the roadside?

Sale by an unregistered breeder or seller violates the 2017 and 2018 Rules.

The buyer is not usually prosecuted, but has almost no remedy when the animal turns out to be sick or underage, and buying sustains the trade the Rules exist to stop. The useful step is the other way round: report the seller to the State Animal Welfare Board, which is the authority those Rules make responsible for registration, with the listing, the address and any photographs. Where animals are kept in conditions that cause suffering, that is also Section 11 of the PCA Act.

Basis
A19Is ear cropping or tail docking legal?

Treat it as mutilation.

Section 11(1)(l) of the PCA Act prohibits mutilating an animal, and cosmetic cropping or docking has no medical justification. The AWBI circular of 2011 and Veterinary Council ethics treat it as professional misconduct, so the veterinarian who performs it can be proceeded against.

Basis
A20Are dog fights legal?

No.

Section 11(1)(m) covers organising, keeping or using premises for animal fighting, and Section 11(1)(n) covers promoting a fight or merely being present at one. Spectators are liable. Where a dog dies, add BNS Section 325.

Basis
A21Can one abandon a dog?

No.

Section 11(1)(i) makes it an offence to abandon an animal in circumstances likely to cause it suffering from starvation or thirst. If the animal dies, the abandonment becomes a killing and BNS Section 325 applies. Surrender to a shelter instead, which is lawful.

Basis
A22Can one keep a dog chained all day?

No.

Section 11(1)(f) specifically prohibits keeping an animal chained on an unreasonably short or heavy chain for an unreasonable period.

Do thisRead with the Section 3 duty of care, prolonged tethering is cruelty on its face and photographs establish it.

Basis
A23What are the rules for transporting dogs?

The Transport of Animals Rules 1978 prescribe crate size, ventilation, water and a veterinary fitness certificate for rail and air. Independently, Section 11(1)(d) of the PCA Act makes conveying an animal in a way that causes unnecessary pain an offence, so a breach of the Rules and a cruelty offence usually arise on the same facts.

Basis
A24Can I take my dog on a train?

Yes.

Indian Railways permits dogs in a first class AC coupe or first class cabin on booking, or in the brake van dog box. Carry the vaccination certificate. Confining an animal in the brake van without water or ventilation remains cruelty regardless of the ticket.

Basis
A25Can I fly with my dog in India?

Yes, in cabin or cargo depending on the airline, with health and vaccination certificates.

Each carrier sets its own crate and weight conditions under DGCA guidance. The Transport of Animals Rules still govern the crate itself.

Basis
A26How do I bring a dog into India from abroad?

You need a no objection certificate from the Animal Quarantine and Certification Service, a microchip, a rabies antibody titre and a health certificate, within the DGFT import conditions. Pets brought as baggage on transfer of residence are permitted subject to those conditions.

Basis
A27Who is responsible for compensation after a dog bite?

There is no statutory scheme yet.

Courts in Kerala and the Punjab and Haryana High Court have awarded compensation against local bodies for failing to run ABC programmes, and the Supreme Court in 2026 discussed fixing liability on defaulting local bodies. The claim lies against the authority that neglected its ABC duty.

Basis
A28What if my pet dog bites someone?

You face civil liability and possible prosecution for negligent conduct with an animal under BNS Section 291, which replaced IPC Section 289. Keep the animal leashed in public and keep vaccination proof. A current anti rabies certificate is the single most useful document you can hold.

Basis
A29Is a leash compulsory in public?

Under most municipal by-laws, yes, and some require muzzling for larger dogs.

This is a by-law question, so the answer is set by your corporation and not by the PCA Act. Non compliance also weakens your position if a bite is alleged.

Basis
A30Can community dogs be sterilised privately, or only through the ABC programme?

No.

Under the ABC Rules 2023, humane capture, surgery and return must be done by an AWBI recognised organisation, with trained handlers and registered veterinarians at a recognised facility. This protects the dog: unauthorised capture is a breach of the Rules and, if it causes pain, an offence under Section 11.

Basis
A31Can police or the municipality pick up my pet dog?

Not a collared, registered pet, which must be returned to you.

A municipality may take in an animal only within its by-laws, for example one that is unowned and unclaimed, or where a veterinary or court process requires it, and the animal’s welfare must be protected throughout.

Do thisKeep the registration and tag on the dog, because identification is what converts this from a dispute into a simple return.

Basis
A32What is the AWBI?

The Animal Welfare Board of India, a statutory body constituted under Section 4 of the PCA Act.

It advises the central government, issues guidelines and advisories, recognises ABC organisations, and appoints Honorary Animal Welfare Officers who can assist at the scene.

Basis
A33Can I report cruelty, and to whom?

Yes.

Go to the police and insist on an FIR under BNS Section 325 read with Section 11 of the PCA Act. Section 325 is cognizable, and Section 173 of the BNSS 2023, which replaced Section 154 of the CrPC, obliges the officer to register information disclosing a cognizable offence. Say both things at the counter: the section, and that it is cognizable. If refused, write to the Superintendent of Police and then move the Magistrate under Section 175(3).

Basis
A34Is it illegal to leave a dog in a parked car?

Yes.

Confining an animal in a space that does not allow it a reasonable opportunity for movement, or keeping it in conditions causing unnecessary suffering, falls under Section 11(1)(e) and Section 11(1)(g). In summer this is also a killing risk, which brings in BNS Section 325.

Basis
A35Are dogs allowed in parks?

Public parks may restrict access by by-law.

A resident society cannot impose a blanket ban on pets using common areas, per AWBI guidance. The distinction matters: a municipal by-law is law, a society circular is not.

Basis
A36Can a landlord refuse tenants with dogs?

A landlord can set lease terms before you sign, and that is a contract question.

Once you are in occupation, the society cannot force you to give up the animal, since that is governed by AWBI guidance and the case law on pets in housing.

Basis
A37Are there rules on dog kennels and boarding?

Boarding and kennelling are treated in several states under the pet shop and breeding registration framework, and AWBI guidance applies to housing, space and veterinary cover. The PCA duty of care in Section 3 applies to the boarder for the whole period the animal is in their charge.

Basis
A38What is the national rabies programme?

The National Rabies Control Programme, together with the National Action Plan for Dog Mediated Rabies Elimination by 2030, which pairs mass dog vaccination with free anti rabies vaccine for bite victims at government hospitals. Vaccinating dogs and protecting people with free vaccine, not displacing dogs, is the strategy the state has committed to.

Basis
A39Is it legal to shoot a dog on a farm?

No.

Killing an animal attracts BNS Section 325 and Section 11 of the PCA Act. Private defence is a narrow exception that requires a real and immediate threat to life, and it is judged after the fact on the facts. Property protection is not a defence.

Basis
A40Can dogs be used in films and advertisements?

Only with prior permission.

Section 22 of the PCA Act restricts exhibition and training of performing animals, and the Performing Animals (Registration) Rules 2001 require registration with the AWBI and a pre shoot no objection certificate. Shooting without it is an offence and the footage is evidence of it.

Basis
A41Is street dog community ownership recognised?

Yes.

The ABC Rules 2023 recognise community animals and the role of caregivers, and give feeders both standing and duties: use the designated spot, keep it clean, and cooperate with sterilisation drives. That recognition is what a feeder cites when an association objects.

Basis
A42Can a municipality label a dog aggressive?

Only through the committee process in the ABC Rules, on evidence.

The 2026 directions require evidence, assessed by qualified veterinary experts, rather than the volume of complaints, and a dog is entitled to humane treatment throughout.

Do thisAsk to see the committee's finding, since a label applied without one is open to challenge.

Basis
A43What if the local body is not doing ABC?

Complain in writing to the Municipal Commissioner and the District Collector, and keep the acknowledgement.

The Supreme Court in 2026 required Chief Secretaries to report district ABC centres to the High Courts, so the default is already on record. Beyond that, a writ petition under Article 226 lies against the authority.

Basis
A44Can I run a private dog shelter?

Yes.

Register as a society or trust, and seek AWBI recognition, which is needed for ABC work and helps with grants. Shelter conditions themselves are governed by the Section 3 duty of care, so overcrowding in a shelter is as much an offence as it is anywhere else.

Basis
A45Is dog meat legal in India?

No.

Dogs are not a food animal under the Food Safety and Standards framework, so slaughter and sale for meat has no licence route and is unlawful. Transport for that purpose also attracts the PCA Act and BNS Section 325.

Basis
A46Are guard dog owners liable for injuries to trespassers?

Generally yes.

Courts expect visible warning and effective control, and negligent keeping attracts BNS Section 291, the successor to IPC Section 289. Signage and secure fencing are the practical defence.

Basis
A47Should I get my dog microchipped?

It is voluntary in most places, mandatory for import and export, and required by some municipal registration schemes.

It is also the most reliable proof of ownership if your dog is picked up or stolen.

Basis
A48What can be done when dogs are overloaded in crates or vehicles?

Section 11(1)(a) covers overloading and Section 11(1)(d) covers conveyance causing unnecessary pain, alongside the Transport of Animals Rules 1978. The vehicle can be stopped and the animals seized, and the animals can be placed in custody pending trial rather than returned to the accused.

Basis
A49Are electric or prong collars banned?

There is no specific prohibition by name.

Use that causes pain or injury is prosecutable under Section 11(1)(a) read with the Section 3 duty of care, and a veterinary report describing the injury is what carries such a complaint.

Basis
A50Where do I find the official texts?

The bare Acts and Rules are on indiacode.nic.in, the ABC Rules 2023 and AWBI guidelines are on awbi.gov.in, and reported judgments are on the Supreme Court and High Court websites. Quote the section, not a summary, whenever it matters.

Basis

Cows & cattle

B1Which law governs cattle slaughter?

Your State's cattle preservation Act, not the PCA Act.

Cattle slaughter is a State subject, so each State has its own Act naming which animals are protected, what age or fitness certificate is needed, and the punishment. Most such Acts carry imprisonment measured in years, far heavier than the PCA Act. Look up your State's Act on indiacode.nic.in and cite its section, because the PCA Act on its own will not carry a slaughter case.

Basis
B2Is cow slaughter banned across India?

No.

It is a State subject under Entry 15 of the State List, so the position differs by State. Most northern and western States impose near total bans; Kerala, West Bengal and most of the North East do not; several fall between, protecting cows but not buffalo or bulls. Article 48 of the Constitution directs States towards prohibition, but it is a directive principle and not directly enforceable.

Basis
B3Can buffalo be slaughtered?

It depends on the State.

Some Acts protect buffalo outright, some only below a stated age, and some not at all. Where an age or fitness condition applies, the Act usually requires a certificate from a named authority before slaughter, and slaughter without that certificate is the offence. Several State Acts also put the burden of proving that a slaughter was lawful on the accused.

Do thisRead your State's Act for the animal list and the certificate rule before you complain.

Basis
B4What is the punishment under a State cattle Act?

It varies by State, but the pattern is the same: imprisonment measured in years, fines in lakhs, more for a repeat offence, and power to seize the animals and the vehicle used. That is the provision to cite in a cattle transport case, not the PCA Act, whose penalties are small. Find the punishment section of your State's Act and quote it in the complaint.

Basis
B5Who can seize cattle being transported illegally?

A police officer not below the rank prescribed in the Act, or an authorised officer, may seize the cattle and the vehicle. Ask that the seizure memo record the number of animals, their condition and the vehicle registration, because that document decides what happens to the animals afterwards.

Basis
B6Where do seized cattle go?

To a gaushala or infirmary.

The Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (Care and Maintenance of Case Property Animals) Rules 2017 allow a Magistrate to give custody of animals seized as case property to an animal welfare organisation pending trial, rather than returning them to the accused. This is the single most useful rule in a live seizure.

Basis
B7Can seized cattle be returned to the accused?

Resist it.

Under the Case Property Animals Rules 2017 the court may entrust the animals to an infirmary or welfare organisation, and returning them to a person facing a cruelty or slaughter charge defeats the purpose of the seizure.

Do thisFile an application citing those Rules as soon as the seizure is made.

Basis
B8How many cattle may be carried in a truck?

The Transport of Animals Rules 1978 set the numbers by vehicle size, and overcrowding is the norm in illegal transport.

Overloading is an offence under Section 11(1)(a) and conveyance causing unnecessary pain under Section 11(1)(d). Count the animals and photograph the deck; the count is the offence.

Basis
B9Is tying cattle legs during transport an offence?

Yes.

Binding legs, roping animals together, or packing them so they cannot stand causes unnecessary pain and falls squarely within Section 11(1)(d) read with the Transport of Animals Rules 1978. It is also the clearest visual evidence available at a checkpost.

Basis
B10What permits are needed to transport cattle between States?

A valid transport permit, a veterinary certificate of fitness for the journey, and compliance with the Transport of Animals Rules 1978. Where the State has a cattle preservation Act, movement for slaughter is separately restricted by it, and many States require a permit for taking cattle out of the State at all. Absence of a fitness certificate is itself a violation you can point to on the spot.

Basis
B11Is cattle smuggling across the border an offence?

Yes, and the Supreme Court in the Gauri Maulekhi litigation directed States to prevent illegal transport of cattle across the border to Nepal for sacrifice. Add the Customs Act and the State cattle preservation law to the PCA charges.

Basis
B12Can a gaushala be prosecuted for neglect?

Yes.

Section 3 places the duty of care on whoever has charge of the animal, and a gaushala has charge. Starvation, denial of water, or leaving carcasses among living animals attracts Section 11(1)(h) and Section 11(1)(j). Registration as a charity is not a shield.

Basis
B13What is phooka or doom dev?

Injecting air or any substance into a cow to make it yield more milk.

Section 12 of the PCA Act creates a separate offence for it, punishable with fine and imprisonment, and the animal can be forfeited to the government. It is one of the few PCA sections with a forfeiture consequence worth invoking.

Basis
B14Is oxytocin use on dairy cattle legal?

No.

Oxytocin for milk letdown is prohibited, its sale is restricted under the Drugs and Cosmetics framework, and administering an injurious drug to an animal is an offence under Section 12 and Section 11(1)(c) of the PCA Act. Seize the vial, since possession on a dairy is the evidence.

Basis
B15Can calves be separated from cows immediately after birth?

There is no provision that prohibits separation by name.

Where separation results in the calf being starved or denied colostrum, Section 11(1)(h) applies, and the Section 3 duty of care runs to the calf independently of its commercial value.

Basis
B16Is it legal to abandon unproductive cattle on the road?

No.

Section 11(1)(i) makes abandonment an offence, and the owner remains responsible. Abandoned cattle that cause accidents also expose the owner to negligence proceedings, so this is one area where owner identification matters.

Basis
B17Who is responsible when abandoned cattle are on the roads?

The owner who abandoned them, if identifiable, and the local body where it has failed to take abandoned cattle into safe care under its by-laws.

High Courts have directed municipalities to move cattle off highways into pounds and shelters, for the animals’ safety as much as road users’, and a writ under Article 226 is the route when the authority does nothing.

Basis
B18Are cattle markets regulated?

Yes, by State market law, municipal by-laws and animal health requirements.

The central Regulation of Livestock Markets Rules 2017 were stayed by the Supreme Court and later withdrawn, so do not cite them as current. Cruelty at a market remains prosecutable under Section 11 regardless.

Basis
B19Is bullock cart racing legal?

It depends on the State and on whether a State amendment survives.

After Animal Welfare Board of India v. A. Nagaraja (2014) such events were held to violate Sections 3 and 11, and several States amended the PCA Act to permit them. A Constitution Bench in 2023 upheld those State amendments as valid. Cruelty during a permitted event is still an offence.

Basis
B20What did the Nagaraja judgment actually hold?

That Article 21 extends beyond human beings, that life for an animal means more than mere survival, that the five internationally recognised freedoms should inform the reading of the PCA Act, and that Sections 3 and 11 must be read with the fundamental duty of compassion in Article 51A(g). It is the strongest authority available in Indian animal law and belongs in any serious petition.

Basis
B21Can cattle be branded with hot irons?

Face branding causes unnecessary pain and is prosecutable under Section 11(1)(a).

AWBI has advised against hot iron branding, and where the practice is used to identify animals there are non injurious alternatives, which removes the necessity defence.

Basis
B22Is castration without anaesthesia an offence?

Section 11(3) exempts certain procedures done in the prescribed manner, but the exemption is read narrowly.

Castration performed crudely or without pain relief, causing avoidable suffering, is prosecutable under Section 11(1)(a) read with Section 3.

Basis
B23What are the rules for slaughterhouses?

The PCA (Slaughter House) Rules 2001 govern the premises, ante mortem inspection and the requirement that an animal be killed out of sight of other animals. Food safety licensing runs in parallel under the FSSA 2006. An unlicensed slaughterhouse is doubly in breach.

Basis
B24Can an animal be slaughtered in front of another animal?

No.

The Slaughter House Rules 2001 require that no animal be slaughtered in sight of another, and that the animal be given rest, water and ante mortem inspection first. Video from inside the premises is the evidence that establishes this.

Basis
B25Is religious slaughter exempt from the PCA Act?

Section 28 of the PCA Act saves the killing of an animal in a manner required by the religion of any community.

The saving covers the method of killing, not the treatment before it, so transport, confinement and handling remain fully prosecutable under Section 11.

Basis
B26Is animal sacrifice at temples legal?

It varies by State, and several States prohibit it by specific legislation.

Where it is not prohibited, cruelty in transporting or confining the animal beforehand remains an offence, and courts have restrained sacrifice in temple premises on public order and welfare grounds.

Basis
B27Can cattle be used for ploughing beyond fixed hours?

Working an animal beyond its capacity, or working an unfit animal, is an offence under Section 11(1)(a) and Section 11(1)(b). There is no universal hour limit, so the test is the condition of the animal, which a veterinary certificate records.

Basis
B28Is it an offence to work a lame or injured bullock?

Yes.

Section 11(1)(b) makes it an offence to employ an animal that is unfit by reason of age, disease, infirmity, wound or sore. This is one of the easiest offences to prove, because the injury is visible and a veterinary opinion settles it.

Basis
B29What is the legal status of a cow shelter's land and funds?

A registered gaushala is a trust or society, so its funds are held on trust for the animals and are subject to charity law and audit. Diversion of funds is a matter for the Charity Commissioner or Registrar, separate from any PCA prosecution for neglect.

Basis
B30Are there rules on dairy hygiene?

Yes, under the FSSA 2006 and the Food Safety and Standards Regulations, which cover licensing, milk handling and adulteration. Animal welfare in the dairy is governed separately by the PCA Act, and a food licence does not answer a cruelty allegation.

Basis
B31Can I file a complaint about a dairy keeping cattle in filth?

Yes.

Keeping an animal in a habitation with insufficient space, light, air or cleanliness is an offence under Section 11(1)(g), and the duty of care in Section 3 applies to the dairy owner.

Do thisReport to the municipal health officer and the police together.

Basis
B32What is the role of the SPCA?

Societies for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals are constituted at district level under rules made under the PCA Act, with the District Collector usually chairing. They can inspect, assist in seizure and maintain infirmaries. Where a district SPCA is defunct, that itself is a matter to raise with the Collector.

Basis
B33Is dehorning cattle legal?

Dehorning done in the prescribed manner is within the Section 11(3) exemption.

Done crudely, causing bleeding and prolonged pain, it falls back into Section 11(1)(a). The manner, not the label, decides it.

Basis
B34Can a cow be tethered in the sun without water?

No.

Denial of water and food is expressly an offence under Section 11(1)(h), and keeping an animal in conditions causing unnecessary suffering falls under Section 11(1)(g).

Do thisPhotograph the water vessel, or its absence.

Basis
B35What happens to cattle rescued in a raid?

They should be examined by a veterinarian, and custody sought under the Case Property Animals Rules 2017 so they go to an infirmary or gaushala rather than back to the accused. Move the application immediately, because delay is what usually returns the animals.

Basis
B36Can the accused claim the cattle as their property?

They will try.

The answer is that the Case Property Animals Rules 2017 exist precisely to keep animals out of the hands of a person facing a cruelty charge during trial, and that Section 29 permits forfeiture of the animal on conviction. Property in a living animal is not absolute.

Basis
B37Is there compensation for cattle culled in a disease outbreak?

Yes, under the PCICDA 2009 framework and the relevant central scheme rates, where culling is ordered to control a notified disease. Rates are revised periodically, so check the current departmental notification rather than an older figure.

Basis
B38Is foot and mouth disease notifiable?

Yes.

Under the PCICDA 2009 a person in charge of an animal must report a notified disease to the prescribed authority. Failure to report is an offence, and it is also what allows an outbreak to spread through a village.

Basis
B39Can cattle be transported in a closed container lorry?

No.

The Transport of Animals Rules 1978 require adequate ventilation and standing space. A closed container without ventilation is both a Rules breach and, on the facts, an offence under Section 11(1)(d), and animals frequently die in transit in them.

Basis
B40Who inspects cattle transport on the highway?

Police, transport department officers, and animal husbandry officials, with AWBI Honorary Animal Welfare Officers able to assist. Where a checkpost waves vehicles through, that inaction is itself a ground for a writ petition against the authority.

Basis
B41Can a private citizen stop a cattle truck?

Do not attempt it.

Call the police control room, note the registration number, location and time, and follow with a written complaint. A citizen who intercepts a vehicle risks criminal proceedings themselves and hands the defence a distraction from the animals.

Basis
B42Is cow vigilantism lawful?

No.

The Supreme Court has directed States to prevent it and to act against those who take the law into their own hands. Assault and mob violence are offences under the BNS regardless of the motive, and vigilantism in practice destroys the prosecution of the underlying transport offence.

Basis
B43Can cattle graze on public land?

Grazing rights depend on State revenue and forest law and on local custom.

Grazing in a reserved forest without authority attracts the Wildlife and Forest Acts. Tethering on a road margin is a municipal matter.

Basis
B44Is bull taming or Jallikattu legal?

It is permitted only where a State amendment to the PCA Act provides for it.

Three States have such amendments, and a Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court upheld them in May 2023. Everywhere else the Nagaraja judgment of 2014 still applies and the event is unlawful. The permission is conditional on compliance with the rules made under it, and cruelty outside those conditions remains an offence.

Basis
B45What is Kambala and is it allowed?

Kambala is traditional buffalo racing.

It was restrained after Nagaraja and then permitted in the one State that amended the PCA Act for it, an amendment the Supreme Court upheld in 2023. It remains unlawful anywhere without such an amendment. Beating, tail biting or use of sharp implements during a race remains an offence under Section 11.

Basis
B46Can cattle be tied up for long hours at a market?

Confinement without space, water or shade is an offence under Section 11(1)(g) and Section 11(1)(h).

Market operators are persons in charge for the purpose of Section 3, so responsibility does not disappear because the animal is in transit between owners.

Basis
B47Is skinning a dead animal in public an offence?

It is regulated by municipal by-laws and by the Slaughter House Rules where the premises are licensed.

Skinning an animal that is still alive is a grave offence under Section 11(1)(l) and BNS Section 325, and this does occur in carcass trade.

Basis
B48What can I do if the police refuse to register an FIR?

Send the complaint in writing to the Superintendent of Police under Section 173(4) of the BNSS 2023.

If that fails, move the Judicial Magistrate under Section 175(3) for a direction to investigate, or file a private complaint under Section 223.

Do thisKeep every acknowledgement.

Basis
B49Can I get an animal examined by a government vet for evidence?

Yes, and you should.

A veterinary certificate describing injuries, body condition and probable cause converts an allegation into a case.

Do thisAsk for it in writing and keep the original, since the prosecution will need it and copies go missing.

Basis
B50Where do I find the official texts on cattle law?

indiacode.nic.in for the PCA Act, the Rules and every State cattle Act, your State gazette for local notifications, dahd.gov.in for disease and transport notifications, and awbi.gov.in for advisories. Cite the section, not a news report.

Basis

Animal husbandry

Dairy, poultry, sheep and goat, pigs, experimentation, wildlife kept captive, and the basics for fisheries.

C1Does the PCA Act apply to farm animals?

Yes, fully.

The definition of animal in Section 2(a) covers any living creature other than a human being, with no exclusion for livestock. Section 3 and Section 11 apply on a farm exactly as they apply in a home, and commercial purpose is not a defence.

Basis
C2Are battery cages for hens legal?

There is no express statutory ban, but confinement that denies an animal reasonable opportunity for movement is an offence under Section 11(1)(e). AWBI has advised States that battery cages violate Section 11, and the Nagaraja reading of the five freedoms supports that position. Draft Layer Rules have been under consideration for years without notification.

Basis
C3How much space must a caged hen have?

No central rule has been notified fixing cage dimensions for layers.

AWBI advisories and draft rules have proposed minimum floor area per bird, and several States have issued directions. Until a rule is notified, the argument runs through Section 11(1)(e) on the facts of the cage.

Basis
C4Is debeaking chicks legal?

Debeaking causes pain and is prosecutable under Section 11(1)(a) and Section 11(1)(l) as mutilation where it is done without justification or without pain relief. AWBI has advised against routine debeaking. The practice is widespread, which does not make it lawful.

Basis
C5Is killing male chicks at hatcheries lawful?

Maceration or suffocation of day old male chicks is killing an animal in an unnecessarily cruel manner and falls within Section 11(1)(l), read with BNS Section 325. There is no notified rule permitting it, so the practice rests on nothing but custom.

Basis
C6Are there rules for transporting poultry?

Yes.

The Transport of Animals Rules 1978 contain a chapter on poultry covering crate space, ventilation and journey conditions. Carrying birds hung by the legs from a vehicle is an offence under Section 11(1)(d) and is the most common violation on the road.

Basis
C7Is carrying live birds hanging upside down an offence?

Yes, clearly.

It causes unnecessary pain and falls under Section 11(1)(a) and Section 11(1)(d), and it breaches the poultry chapter of the Transport of Animals Rules 1978.

Do thisPhotograph the vehicle and the birds together with the registration plate.

Basis
C8Can chickens be slaughtered in the open market?

Slaughter must be at a licensed premises under the Slaughter House Rules 2001 and the FSSA licensing framework.

Killing a bird in sight of other birds waiting in the crate breaches the Rules and Section 11, and that is exactly how most open market slaughter is done.

Basis
C9Do meat shops need a licence?

Yes, under the FSSA 2006 and the local body's trade licence regime, with health and hygiene conditions attached.

An unlicensed shop can be closed by the food safety officer, which is often faster than a cruelty prosecution.

Basis
C10Is stunning before slaughter required?

The Slaughter House Rules 2001 contemplate stunning, subject to the Section 28 saving for religious slaughter.

Where a method is not covered by that saving, failure to stun that causes prolonged suffering is prosecutable under Section 11.

Basis
C11Are goats and sheep covered by animal welfare law?

Yes, on the same footing as any other animal under Section 2(a).

Transport, confinement, water and handling are governed by Section 11 and by the Transport of Animals Rules 1978, and the sheep and goat chapter sets journey conditions.

Basis
C12How many goats can be carried in a vehicle?

The Transport of Animals Rules 1978 prescribe floor space per animal.

In practice vehicles are loaded to double the permitted number, and the count against the deck area is what makes the offence provable rather than arguable.

Basis
C13Is tying a goat's legs for transport an offence?

Yes, under Section 11(1)(d) as conveyance causing unnecessary pain, and as a breach of the Rules.

Animals must be able to stand and lie in a natural position for the journey.

Basis
C14Are pig farms regulated?

They fall under the PCA Act for welfare, the FSSA for meat, State pollution control law for effluent, and local body licensing for the premises. Where a farm is polluting, the pollution control board route often moves faster than a welfare complaint.

Basis
C15Can pigs be kept in sty conditions without space?

Keeping any animal in a habitation with insufficient space, light, air or cleanliness is an offence under Section 11(1)(g). Farrowing crates that prevent an animal from turning also engage Section 11(1)(e).

Basis
C16Does animal welfare law cover fish?

The definition of animal in Section 2(a) covers any living creature, and courts have not excluded fish.

In practice enforcement is thin, and fisheries are governed mainly by State fisheries law, the Marine Fishing Regulation Acts and FSSA standards.

Basis
C17Is live fish transport regulated?

There is no dedicated chapter in the Transport of Animals Rules for fish.

Transport that causes avoidable suffering can still be pressed under Section 11(1)(d), and food safety conditions apply under the FSSA 2006.

Basis
C18Are ornamental fish and aquarium trade regulated?

Import of ornamental species is controlled under the DGFT policy and quarantine requirements, and species listed under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 cannot be traded at all. Check the schedules before buying anything wild caught.

Basis
C19What is the PCICDA 2009?

The Prevention and Control of Infectious and Contagious Diseases in Animals Act 2009.

It makes listed diseases notifiable, obliges the person in charge of an animal to report them, and empowers the government to declare controlled areas, restrict movement and order vaccination or culling.

Basis
C20Which animal diseases are notifiable?

Those listed in the Schedule to the PCICDA 2009, which includes foot and mouth disease, anthrax, brucellosis, rabies, avian influenza and others. The Schedule is amended from time to time, so check the current notification.

Basis
C21What happens during an avian influenza outbreak?

The government declares an infected zone, restricts movement and orders culling and disposal under the PCICDA 2009 and the departmental action plan. Culling must still be carried out by methods that avoid unnecessary suffering, and Section 11 does not switch off during an outbreak.

Basis
C22Is compensation payable for culled poultry?

Yes, at rates fixed by the central and State action plans for the disease concerned.

Claims are made through the animal husbandry department, and rates change, so rely on the current notification rather than a published figure.

Basis
C23Are antibiotics in animal feed regulated?

Yes.

The FSSA framework prescribes maximum residue limits in food of animal origin, and the use of antibiotics as growth promoters has been restricted by notification. Residue testing is the enforcement tool, and it sits with the food safety authority.

Basis
C24Is hormone use in dairy or poultry legal?

Oxytocin for milk letdown is prohibited and its sale is restricted.

Administering an injurious substance to an animal is an offence under Section 11(1)(c) and Section 12 of the PCA Act, quite apart from food safety consequences.

Basis
C25Do animal farms need environmental clearance?

Large units require consent to establish and consent to operate from the State pollution control board, and effluent and waste disposal are regulated. A farm operating without consent can be directed to close, which is often the effective remedy.

Basis
C26Can a farm be shut for cruelty?

The PCA Act does not provide a closure power by itself, but the licensing authority, the food safety authority and the pollution control board each can. A cruelty complaint filed alongside a licensing complaint is far more effective than either alone.

Basis
C27Are there rules for animal markets?

Markets are regulated by State market committee law and municipal by-laws, with animal health conditions under the PCICDA 2009. The central Livestock Market Rules 2017 were stayed and withdrawn, so do not rely on them.

Basis
C28Can animals be sold at a market without health certification?

Movement and sale of animals from a controlled area without certification breaches the PCICDA 2009.

Separately, offering for sale an animal that is diseased or suffering is an offence under Section 11(1)(k).

Basis
C29Is animal experimentation regulated?

Yes.

Section 15 of the PCA Act constitutes the Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals, and the Breeding of and Experiments on Animals Rules 1998 govern institutional ethics committees, registration and permitted procedures.

Basis
C30Is cosmetics testing on animals banned in India?

Yes.

The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules were amended to prohibit testing of cosmetics on animals and to prohibit the import of cosmetics tested on animals. India was among the first countries in Asia to do this.

Basis
C31Can students dissect animals in school?

Dissection has been phased out in school curricula and restricted in undergraduate courses by UGC and MCI directions, with alternatives prescribed. Institutional use of animals otherwise requires CPCSEA registration.

Basis
C32Are circuses allowed to use animals?

The use of most wild animals in circuses has been prohibited by notification under Section 22 of the PCA Act, and N.R.

Nair v. Union of India upheld that prohibition. Any performing animal requires registration under the Performing Animals (Registration) Rules 2001.

Basis
C33Are elephants allowed in temples and processions?

Captive elephants require ownership certificates under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 and must comply with AWBI and Project Elephant guidelines on chaining, work hours and transport. Courts have repeatedly restrained processions where those conditions are not met.

Basis
C34Can wild animals be kept as pets?

No.

Possession of a scheduled wild animal without authority is an offence under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, punishable with imprisonment. This includes many birds, tortoises and reptiles sold openly in markets.

Basis
C35Is keeping Indian birds in cages legal?

No, for indigenous species.

Trapping, keeping or trading indigenous birds is prohibited under the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, and the Delhi High Court in People for Animals v. Md Mohazzim held that birds have a right to fly and cannot be caged for trade.

Basis
C36What is the penalty under the Wildlife Act?

For a Schedule I animal, imprisonment of not less than three years extending to seven years with fine, under Section 51.

That is far heavier than the PCA Act, which is why species identification at the scene matters so much.

Basis
C37Who enforces the Wildlife Act?

The forest department, through the Chief Wildlife Warden and authorised officers, with police assistance.

Wildlife offences are cognizable and non bailable for the higher schedules, and seized animals become government property under Section 39.

Basis
C38Can I keep bees or is apiculture regulated?

Beekeeping is promoted under the National Beekeeping and Honey Mission and honey is regulated for adulteration under the FSSA. Destroying wild colonies by burning can attract the Wildlife Act where a protected species is involved.

Basis
C39Are working bullocks in agriculture protected?

Yes, by Section 11(1)(a) and Section 11(1)(b), which cover overloading, beating and employing an unfit animal.

State bullock cart rules also prescribe load limits in some places.

Basis
C40Is there a legal duty to provide shade and water?

Yes, through Section 3, which requires the person in charge to take reasonable measures for the wellbeing of the animal, and through Section 11(1)(h) on failure to provide food and water. Denial of water in the Indian summer is a straightforward offence.

Basis
C41Can I complain about a neighbour's neglected livestock?

Yes.

Report to the police, the district SPCA and the animal husbandry department, citing Section 3 and the applicable clause of Section 11.

Do thisAsk for a veterinary examination, because the certificate is what carries the complaint.

Basis
C42What is an Honorary Animal Welfare Officer?

A person appointed by the AWBI who can visit premises, assist the police at a seizure and report violations.

The appointment carries no police power, but it gives standing and it usually gets the door opened.

Basis
C43Can an FIR be filed against a company or a farm owner?

Yes.

Where the offence is by a company, the persons in charge of and responsible to the company for the conduct of its business can be proceeded against. Name the proprietor or the responsible manager, not just the establishment.

Basis
C44Is animal fat or by-product processing regulated?

Rendering plants require pollution control consent, food safety licensing where the product enters the food chain, and local body permission. Cruelty in the supply of animals to such a plant remains separately prosecutable.

Basis
C45Are leather tanneries connected to animal law?

The welfare offences arise upstream, in transport and slaughter, and are prosecuted under the PCA Act and the State cattle preservation law. Tannery operation itself is governed by pollution control law.

Basis
C46Can I object to an animal sacrifice at a local festival?

Yes.

Several States prohibit sacrifice by specific legislation, and where that applies the local police can act. Where it does not, the transport and confinement of the animal beforehand remain prosecutable under Section 11.

Basis
C47Are there subsidies tied to welfare compliance?

Schemes under the National Livestock Mission and the Rashtriya Gokul Mission carry conditions on housing and disease control. A unit prosecuted for cruelty can be raised with the sanctioning authority, which is an underused pressure point.

Basis
C48Who regulates veterinary practice?

The Veterinary Council of India under the Indian Veterinary Council Act 1984, with State veterinary councils registering practitioners. Professional misconduct, including performing cosmetic mutilation, is a disciplinary matter for the council.

Basis
C49Can a vet refuse to treat an injured street animal?

There is no statutory duty to treat, but veterinary ethics require humane handling and first aid in an emergency, and a government veterinary hospital is expected to attend to an animal brought in. Refusal can be raised with the State council and the department.

Basis
C50Where do I find the official texts on husbandry law?

indiacode.nic.in for the PCA Act, the PCICDA 2009 and the Wildlife Act, dahd.gov.in for disease notifications and scheme rates, fssai.gov.in for food standards, and awbi.gov.in for advisories.

Horses & working equines

Horses, ponies, donkeys and mules, including cart, pilgrimage and tourism animals.

D1Which law protects working horses and donkeys?

The PCA Act 1960.

Section 3 places the duty of care on the owner or driver, and Section 11(1)(a) and Section 11(1)(b) cover overloading, beating and employing an animal unfit by reason of age, disease, wound or sore. Working equines are the animals most often visibly unfit, which makes Section 11(1)(b) the practical charge.

Basis
D2Are there specific rules for draught animals?

Yes.

The Prevention of Cruelty to Draught and Pack Animals Rules 1965 prescribe maximum loads, rest, and prohibit working an animal in unfit condition or in extreme heat. A breach of these Rules is an offence under the parent Act.

Basis
D3What is the maximum load a horse cart may carry?

The Draught and Pack Animals Rules 1965 fix limits by animal and vehicle type, including limits on the number of persons in a passenger cart. Overloading beyond those limits is an offence under Section 11(1)(a) read with the Rules.

Basis
D4Can a horse be worked in the afternoon heat?

The 1965 Rules restrict working draught animals during the hottest part of the day and require rest and water.

Working an animal in conditions that cause it to collapse is an offence under Section 11(1)(a) and Section 11(1)(b).

Basis
D5Is it an offence to work a horse with harness sores?

Yes, and it is one of the clearest cases available.

Section 11(1)(b) makes it an offence to employ an animal that is unfit by reason of wound or sore.

Do thisPhotograph the sore under the harness and get a veterinary note; that pair of documents makes the case.

Basis
D6Are tonga and horse cart operators licensed?

Licensing is by municipal by-law, and many corporations require registration of the vehicle and the animal.

An unlicensed operator can be dealt with by the local body more quickly than by prosecution, so raise both.

Basis
D7Can horses be used for weddings and processions?

Yes, subject to welfare conditions and to municipal permission.

Overloading a horse with riders, working it through the night, or using a frightened animal in a crowd with fireworks engages Section 11(1)(a) and the 1965 Rules.

Basis
D8Are fireworks around horses an offence?

Causing an animal unnecessary pain or suffering through terror falls within Section 11(1)(a), and the environmental noise rules restrict firecracker use by time and decibel. Injuries caused by a bolting horse also expose the handler to negligence proceedings.

Basis
D9Is horse riding on the beach regulated?

Yes, through district administration permission and Coastal Regulation Zone conditions, with local bodies restricting hours and rider loads. Welfare complaints still go under the PCA Act and the 1965 Rules.

Basis
D10Are joyride ponies at hill stations regulated?

By State tourism and local body rules on registration, load and rest, and by the 1965 Rules.

Repeated ascents without rest, and working animals with untreated hoof injuries, are the standard violations.

Basis
D11What is glanders and why does it matter?

A notifiable equine disease under the PCICDA 2009.

On confirmation the government can restrict movement, order testing, and direct culling of positive animals, with compensation payable at notified rates.

Basis
D12Is compensation payable when a horse is culled for glanders?

Yes, at rates notified by the department, historically lower for mules and donkeys than for horses.

Rates are revised, so confirm the current departmental rate rather than relying on an older figure.

Basis
D13Can horse movement be restricted during an outbreak?

Yes.

Under the PCICDA 2009 the government may declare a controlled area and prohibit movement of equines into or out of it. Moving an animal in breach is an offence and spreads the outbreak.

Basis
D14Are horse races legal?

Yes, and they are separately regulated by State racing and betting legislation and by turf authority rules.

Cruelty during racing or training, including excessive whipping, remains an offence under Section 11 of the PCA Act.

Basis
D15Is whipping a racehorse an offence?

Beating an animal so as to cause unnecessary pain is an offence under Section 11(1)(a).

Racing rules cap whip use, and a breach of those rules is evidence that the pain caused was unnecessary rather than incidental.

Basis
D16Can horses be doped for performance?

No.

Administering an injurious drug to an animal is an offence under Section 11(1)(c), and racing authorities prohibit it and test for it. Section 12 also covers the administration of substances to alter an animal's condition.

Basis
D17Are horse fights or animal fights legal?

No.

Organising, keeping premises for, or being present at an animal fight is an offence under Section 11(1)(m) and Section 11(1)(n). Spectators are liable, which is the fact most people organising such events do not expect.

Basis
D18Can horses perform in circuses or films?

Only with registration under the Performing Animals (Registration) Rules 2001 and, for a shoot, a prior AWBI no objection certificate. Section 22 of the PCA Act restricts exhibition and training of performing animals generally.

Basis
D19What are the transport rules for horses?

The Transport of Animals Rules 1978 contain a chapter for equines covering partitioning, headroom, footing and fitness certification. Transporting a horse loose in an open truck without partitions is a breach and usually injures the animal.

Basis
D20Can a horse be transported without a fitness certificate?

No.

The 1978 Rules require a certificate from a veterinary practitioner that the animal is fit to travel. Absence of the certificate is the easiest violation to establish at a checkpost, because it is a document that either exists or does not.

Basis
D21Is donkey slaughter legal in India?

Donkeys are not a food animal under the FSSA framework, so there is no licence route for slaughter or sale of the meat.

Killing them attracts BNS Section 325 and the PCA Act, and the trade in donkey hide for export has been a recurring enforcement problem.

Basis
D22Is the donkey hide trade regulated?

Slaughter for hide has no lawful licence route, so the trade operates outside the FSSA framework entirely.

Prosecute it as killing under BNS Section 325 with the PCA Act, and pursue transport violations alongside.

Basis
D23Are donkeys used in brick kilns protected?

Yes, by Section 11(1)(a) and Section 11(1)(b) and by the Draught and Pack Animals Rules 1965 on load and rest.

Kiln work also raises labour and pollution issues, and a joint complaint to the labour department and the pollution board carries more weight.

Basis
D24Can a mule be made to carry construction material up a hill?

Only within the load limits in the 1965 Rules, with rest and water.

Loads that cause the animal to stumble or to bleed at the withers are an offence under Section 11(1)(a) read with those Rules.

Basis
D25Is hobbling or tight tethering of equines an offence?

Keeping an animal chained or tethered on an unreasonably short or heavy chain for an unreasonable period is an offence under Section 11(1)(f). Hobbling that causes injury also engages Section 11(1)(a).

Basis
D26What if a horse is abandoned when it stops working?

Abandonment in circumstances likely to cause suffering is an offence under Section 11(1)(i), and the owner remains liable. Older equines are abandoned routinely, and the ownership trail through the municipal cart licence is how you identify the owner.

Basis
D27Can I rescue an abandoned or collapsed horse?

Call the police and the animal husbandry department, and ask for a veterinary examination on the spot.

Seek custody under the Case Property Animals Rules 2017 if the animal is seized, so it does not return to the person who abandoned it.

Basis
D28Who takes custody of a seized horse?

The Magistrate may entrust animals seized as case property to an infirmary, gaushala or recognised animal welfare organisation under the Case Property Animals Rules 2017, pending trial. Apply immediately, because the default is otherwise return on bond.

Basis
D29Is there a legal duty to provide farriery and hoof care?

Section 3 requires the person in charge to take reasonable measures to ensure the animal's wellbeing, and overgrown or cracked hooves that lame an animal make it unfit to work under Section 11(1)(b). Neglect of hoof care is therefore actionable through both.

Basis
D30Are equine dental and veterinary checks required?

There is no fixed statutory schedule, but the Section 3 duty of care covers routine health, and a working animal with untreated dental disease loses condition and becomes unfit within Section 11(1)(b).

Basis
D31Can horses be kept in a stable without ventilation?

No.

Keeping an animal in a habitation with insufficient space, light, air or cleanliness is an offence under Section 11(1)(g). Ammonia burn and respiratory disease from unmucked stables are the usual consequences and the usual evidence.

Basis
D32Is branding or firing of horses legal?

Firing, the application of a hot iron to treat lameness, causes pain without established benefit and is prosecutable under Section 11(1)(a) and as mutilation under Section 11(1)(l). Veterinary bodies advise against it.

Basis
D33Are nose ropes and bits that cause injury an offence?

Equipment that cuts, ulcerates or causes bleeding causes unnecessary pain and falls under Section 11(1)(a) read with Section 3. Photograph the mouth or nose and the tack together.

Basis
D34Can equines be used to carry pilgrims on temple routes?

Yes, subject to load and rest limits under the 1965 Rules and to any conditions set by the shrine board or district administration. Courts have imposed rest periods and load limits on specific pilgrimage routes where welfare failed.

Basis
D35Who inspects working equines?

Municipal veterinary officers, the animal husbandry department, the district SPCA, AWBI Honorary Animal Welfare Officers, and the police. Where a municipality licenses carts but never inspects them, that gap is itself worth putting in writing.

Basis
D36What is the role of the district SPCA for equines?

It can inspect, assist in seizure and run or refer to an infirmary.

Section 35 of the PCA Act deals with animals sent to an infirmary for treatment and care, which is the route for a collapsed working animal.

Basis
D37Can a horse owner be made to pay for treatment?

Where an animal is sent to an infirmary, the cost of its care can be recovered from the owner under the framework in Section 35 and the Case Property Animals Rules. Ask the court to fix maintenance charges at the time custody is granted.

Basis
D38Is there a minimum age for working a horse?

The 1965 Rules and welfare guidance treat immature animals as unfit for draught work, and employing an animal unfit by reason of age is an offence under Section 11(1)(b). Age is established by dentition, which a veterinarian can certify.

Basis
D39Are equine breeding and sale regulated?

There is no equine equivalent of the dog breeding rules.

Breeding is supported under the National Livestock Mission, and the National Research Centre on Equines sets technical standards. Welfare in breeding premises falls under Sections 3 and 11.

Basis
D40Are there government schemes for equine welfare?

The National Livestock Mission supports equine breeding and development, and departmental programmes cover glanders surveillance. Scheme conditions can be raised with the sanctioning authority where a beneficiary is prosecuted for cruelty.

Basis
D41Is horse meat legal in India?

Horses are not a food animal under the FSSA framework, so there is no lawful route to slaughter or sell horse meat.

Killing attracts BNS Section 325 alongside the PCA Act.

Basis
D42Are rodeo type events legal?

Animal fighting is prohibited under Section 11(1)(m) and Section 11(1)(n).

Events that involve provoking, roping or throwing animals cause unnecessary pain within Section 11(1)(a), and any permitted event runs only on the terms of the permission.

D43Can horses be exhibited at fairs?

Yes, subject to the Performing Animals Rules 2001 where they perform, and to welfare conditions on tethering, water and shade under Section 11. Large fairs are where equine disease also spreads, so PCICDA conditions apply.

D44What happens if a working horse dies on the road?

Ask for a post mortem before disposal, because cause of death is the whole case.

Where death follows from overwork, denial of water or an untreated wound, the charge is BNS Section 325 with Section 11 of the PCA Act, not a municipal carcass removal matter.

Yes, and you should.

No.

Yes.

What changed

The 2025 to 26 position.

Five developments that make older advice wrong. The first three are the ones most often got backwards in a police station.

Supreme Court, 19 May 2026

The ABC Rules remain the framework for humane management of community dogs. Dogs from schools, hospitals and similar premises are to be given safe placement in designated shelters, humane euthanasia is confined to three grounds assessed by veterinary experts, and every district must have a working ABC centre.

Breed bans have no legal footing

The central letter of March 2024 naming 23 breeds was set aside or stayed by six High Courts within weeks. An advisory is not law, and no valid national breed ban exists.

PCA penalties are still token, so do not stop there

The fine under Section 11 remains 10 to 50 rupees, and the 2022 amendment is unenacted. Charge BNS Section 325 alongside it, which carries up to five years for killing or maiming.

BNS 2023 replaced the IPC

Killing or maiming an animal moved from IPC 428 and 429 to Section 325 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita. An FIR still drafted under the IPC sections is drafted under a repealed law.

Your State's cattle Act

Cattle slaughter is a State subject and every State's Act differs in which animals it protects and how heavily. All of them are far heavier than the PCA Act, and the State Act is the provision to lead with in a cattle transport case.

Filing a complaint

Name the section.

An FIR that says only "cruelty to animals" invites a token fine. One that names the provision, attaches a veterinary certificate and asks for custody of the animal is a different document.

Charge both

Section 11 of the PCA Act carries a fine of 10 to 50 rupees. Section 325 of the BNS 2023 carries up to five years for killing or maiming. Ask for both on the same facts.

If the FIR is refused

Section 173 of the BNSS 2023 obliges registration where the information discloses a cognizable offence. Escalate in writing to the Superintendent under Section 173(4), then move the Magistrate under Section 175(3).

Keep the animal safe

Apply at once under the Care and Maintenance of Case Property Animals Rules 2017 so a seized animal goes to an infirmary rather than back to the accused while the trial runs.

Read the bare Act

The Acts and Rules are free at indiacode.nic.in, and the ABC Rules and advisories at awbi.gov.in. Quote the section, never a summary.
