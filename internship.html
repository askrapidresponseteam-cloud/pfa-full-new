Virtual internship

Eight weeks for the animals of your town.

Real work, done where you live. Help at a shelter PFA knows, learn the law that protects animals, find a lawyer who will stand up for them, and bring your town onto the Rapid Response network.

The work

Five core tasks. Three you choose.

Like a degree: the five core tasks are for everyone, and you choose three electives from seven. Each target is the least; going past it counts. None of them starts anything an animal comes to depend on, so no animal is ever worse off when the eight weeks end.

Everything is backed by proof. You send it to PFA on the internship portal below, and PFA signs each task off when it checks out.

CoreAll five, for everyone

Swipe to see all five.

  1. 01

    100people

    Bring your town onto the RRT

    Grow the Rapid Response network in your district, so someone near is there when an animal needs help.

    How it works
    • Your own referral code counts each person who joins
    • Neighbours, college friends, feeders, local vets
    • Proof: the count on your code, read by PFA
  2. 02

    4laws

    Learn the law

    Know what protects animals, and how to use it.

    How it works
    • Section 325 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita
    • The Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960
    • The ABC Rules, 2023, and the right to feed community animals
    • Proof: the online test, passed
  3. 03

    1lawyer

    Find a lawyer

    A lawyer in your district who will take up cases of cruelty to animals.

    How it works
    • Meet them, and ask whether they will act for animals, and on what terms
    • Proof: their name, chambers and number, confirmed by a call from PFA
    • They join the PFA list, so the next case in your district has someone to turn to
  4. 04

    1film

    A film for The Wall

    One short film that teaches something: informative, really well shot, made to change how people see animals.

    How it works
    • One good film is worth more than many quick ones
    • Sent from The Wall; PFA chooses what goes up
    • Your own footage, with permission from anyone in it; no graphic injury, and never an animal disturbed for a shot
  5. 05

    1district

    Map your town or district

    The vets, government veterinary centres, shelters, ABC centres, animal ambulances and feeding spots near you.

    How it works
    • Each place's name, number, hours and address, checked by a call
    • Every government veterinary centre photographed inside and outside, as it is on the day: never staged, tidied or posed
    • Feeding spots already kept by someone near you, located with the feeder's agreement; held by PFA, never published
    • The rest goes into the PFA help directory, so the next person finds help faster
    • Proof: the sheet and the dated photographs, sent on the portal and spot-checked by PFA

ElectivesChoose three of these seven

Swipe to see all seven.

  1. 06

    20hours

    Help at a shelter PFA knows

    Hands-on help alongside the shelter's own staff, who care for the animals before you come and after you go.

    How it works
    • Cleaning, walking and socialising, as the staff ask
    • Helping on treatment days
    • Proof: hours signed off by the shelter or the nearest PFA unit
  2. 07

    1article

    Write for your local paper

    An awareness article in a newspaper where you live, in its own language: the law, sterilisation, first aid, or living alongside community animals.

    How it works
    • Written by you, in the paper's own language
    • Reviewed and passed by the PFA team before you send it to the paper
    • Proof: the published page, a clipping or a link, with its date
  3. 08

    1RTI

    Ask your town about its dogs

    An application under the Right to Information Act to your municipality: how many dogs it sterilised and vaccinated last year, and what it spent.

    How it works
    • PFA gives you the questions and the format
    • Filed online or by post, for a small fee (₹10 in most states)
    • Proof: the acknowledgement number; the reply, when it comes, goes to PFA
  4. 09

    3films

    Nominate three films for CineKind

    Find three films that leave the frame kinder than they found it, and put each in front of the CineKind awards committee.

    How it works
    • Films that show animals truthfully, made without harming one
    • Each nominated on the CineKind page, with a line on why it qualifies
    • Proof: the three nomination numbers
  5. 10

    1talk

    Give one talk

    At a school, college or housing society, on kindness and the law.

    How it works
    • A ready deck and talking points from PFA
    • Twenty minutes, then questions
    • Proof: a photograph, and a note from the host with the number who came
  6. 11

    1module

    Animal first aid

    What to do in the first minutes, until help arrives.

    How it works
    • Approaching a frightened or injured animal safely
    • Bleeding, heat stroke and road accidents
    • When to call, and whom; never treating on your own
    • Proof: the module test, passed
  7. 12

    1story

    Write your story

    One real story from your eight weeks, for the PFA site.

    How it works
    • A shelter animal, a lawyer, a talk, an answer from your town
    • Four to six hundred words, with photographs
    • The best are published on the PFA site

Week by week

How the eight weeks go.

Each week has a focus, so the work builds. Send the proof as each task is done, not all at the end.

  1. The law and the RRTLearn the laws, take the test, and start bringing your town onto the RRT.
  2. Choose your threePick your three electives, and begin the first.
  3. Know your districtMap every vet, shelter, ABC centre and feeding spot, and photograph the government vet centres as they are.
  4. A lawyer for animalsFind and meet one in your district.
  5. Plan your filmThe idea, the script, then the shoot, for The Wall.
  6. Your electivesThe article, the talk, the CineKind films: whichever three you chose, done.
  7. Close out100 on the RRT, the film sent, every proof on the portal.
  8. Final reviewThe last proofs checked by PFA, and the certificate issued.

At the end

A certificate, earned.

It is given when all eight sign-offs are in: the five core tasks and your three electives. Those who go well past the targets are invited to lead the next batch in their state, or to join a PFA unit.

Good to know

Open what you need.

Who can apply?

Anyone in India aged 18 or over, and those aged 16 or 17 with a parent's written consent. Students, working people, homemakers. No experience with animals is needed.

How much time does it take?

About 6 to 8 hours a week for eight weeks, when it suits you inside each week.

Why are feeding and adoption not tasks?

Because no animal must ever be worse off when an internship ends. Animals come to rely on whoever feeds them, and suffer when that person stops; a home found in a hurry can fail, and the animal pays for it. So every task here helps animals without making any of them depend on you. Finding the spots where someone already feeds animals is part of mapping your district; starting one is not. If you already feed animals, keep going: that is your own commitment, outside the internship.

How is the work checked?

Every task is backed by proof, sent on the internship portal and signed off by PFA. Nothing counts on your word alone.

  • Your own RRT referral code, so every person you bring is counted.
  • The online law test.
  • The lawyer's name, chambers and number, confirmed by a call from PFA.
  • Your film, sent from The Wall.
  • The district sheet and the photographs of the government vet centres, spot-checked by PFA.
  • For the electives: hours signed off by the shelter, the article as printed, the RTI acknowledgement number, the host's note on your talk, the three CineKind nomination numbers, the first aid test.
Can I do more than three electives?

Yes. Three are needed for the certificate; the rest count too. Each target is the least, and those who go well past them are asked to lead the next batch in their state.

The safety rules
  • Never handle an aggressive or badly injured animal without a trained person there.
  • Never confront anyone you suspect of cruelty. Report it, step back, and let PFA guide you.
  • Shelter work only at shelters PFA knows and has checked.
  • Interns under 18 work only with a parent's consent and never alone at night.
  • Take on no animal's care you cannot keep up: no starting a feeding spot, no fostering and no adoptions as part of the internship.
Is there a shelter near me?

Very likely. Find one PFA knows in the PFA units directory. If there is none within reach, choose another elective: shelter hours are one of seven.

What is the RRT?

The Rapid Response network: people in each district who help one another when an animal needs help. You bring people from your town onto it with your own referral code.

Is it paid? Is there a fee?

Neither. It is free to join and unpaid. What you take away is the work you did, the people you met, and the certificate.

An animal needs help today.

Do not wait. Call the nearest PFA unit, and see first aid while help comes.
